CBT Workplace Guide · 4 Min Read

Managing Anxiety at Work: 4 Boundary Phrases

When high workplace demands intersect with generalized anxiety, our instinct is often to over-accommodate out of fear of conflict or judgment. Here are four therapist-vetted phrases to hold boundaries with confidence:

1. For sudden urgent requests:
"I would love to help, but I'm currently committed to finishing [Project A] today. Should we deprioritize that, or review this tomorrow morning?"
2. For feeling pressured on the spot:
"Let me digest that and circle back with thoughtful feedback by 3:00 PM."
3. For end-of-day workload:
"I'm wrapping up for the day to stay fresh for tomorrow's deliverable. I'll pick this up first thing at 9 AM."

Remember: A boundary is not a refusal to collaborate; it is the structure that makes sustainable collaboration possible.

Understanding Thought Traps (Cognitive Distortions)

Automatic thoughts are reflex responses our brain generates. When stressed, our minds gravitate toward cognitive distortions:

1. Catastrophizing

Assuming the absolute worst-case scenario will happen, despite lack of evidence.

2. All-or-Nothing Thinking

Seeing situations in black-and-white categories. "If I'm not perfect, I failed."

3. Emotional Reasoning

Believing that because you feel anxious, danger must truly be present.

Next time you observe one of these, ask yourself: "What is the actual evidence for and against this thought?"
